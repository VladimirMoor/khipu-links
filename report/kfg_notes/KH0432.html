Note by Vladimir Muravev:

A nine-cord row form shared by khipus from four places: the first pendant is the largest, the second small (0.05–0.15 of the first), the third and fourth marked by another colour. It occurs on UR1136, UR1140 (Nazca, Berlin VA16635–6), UR223 and UR194 (Ica), UR085 (Museo Temple Radicati), UR235 (Berlin VA66830), KH0698, HP030 (Pachacamac) and AS038 (Lima); the same columns are large and small on all of them, though the hands differ (Z and S knots).

A copy of rows of the Nazca khipus UR1136 and UR1140: group 1 = UR1136 group 5 (100, 5, 16/14, 19, 19/20, 20, 27, 11), group 2 = UR1140 group 16 (53, 8, 11, 19/17, 25, 24, 12, 21/20), group 3 = UR1140 group 6 read backwards (79/80, 9, 23/21, 15/14, 10, 26/25, 26, 36, 32), group 4 = the nine values of UR1140 group 16 in another order, with the marked W:KB cords holding 19 and 11 as the marked cords of UR1140 do. Each match is unique among 1612 rows of 7–11 pendants in the corpus. Knotted SSZ, the Nazca khipus ZZZ. Urton (1994, p. 298) discussed this khipu in his study of knot direction; we have not seen whether he noted the copy.
