Note by Vladimir Muravev:

Provenance suggestion. L. Leland Locke (1923, The Ancient Quipu, Plate XL and p. 29) shows Quipu No. 32 "from Pachacamac, University of Pennsylvania Museum" and notes that a red thread is wrapped about each strand above the knots. This matches KH0019: all nine pendants are recorded as W with a short RM band at 2.5–3.0 cm and each carries 70 (one cluster of seven single knots); no other khipu in the database has this feature on more than two cords. The photograph shows about ten strands with a wrapped band at the top and one long run of single knots on each. We suggest Pachacamac as the provenance (probably Uhle's excavations, 1896). The museum number may need a check: the Penn Museum lists SA4034 as a ten-cord quipu in two groups from the Nazca district (Farabee 1923), which looks like AS020.
