Note by Vladimir Muravev:

This is one of the eight OKR khipus whose pendants are gathered in bundles wrapped with coloured thread (canutos); such khipus are usually assigned to the Wari tradition (Urton; Cherkinsky & Urton 2014 radiocarbon-date three to AD 780–1024). In the OKR the canuto field marks exactly the khipus whose long knots keep nearly the same number of turns: among 120 khipus with at least 40 long knots the eight rank 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 17 and 18 by the spread of turns. On them almost every cluster is a single long knot of 4–7 turns, there are no subsidiaries and no knotless pendants, the long knots are all of one direction (S in the OKR records), and the knots of a bundle sit at similar heights, as if each bundle was knotted together. Reading these knots as decimal digits would be wrong.

Camelid pendants; 54 wrapped ends; bundles mostly of four (15 of 20); knots about 13 cm apart; turns sd 0.77.
