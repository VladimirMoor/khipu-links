Note by Vladimir Muravev:

The earliest date among the Inka-style khipus of Cherkinsky & Urton (2014), VA 42593, 1390–1423 AD (597 ± 18 BP), belongs to this khipu, and the paper cites it for decimal khipus by the start of the Late Horizon. Its knotting is not the usual decimal one: 218 of 226 cords carry exactly three clusters, long knots of up to 13 turns stand in the top position as well, and there are about 120 spiral knots, the same three-slot format as UR050 of the canuto tradition. The date may therefore belong to a local long-knot way of recording (perhaps pre-Inka Ychsma, as Frank Salomon has suggested for anomalous Pachacamac khipus) rather than to decimal writing.
