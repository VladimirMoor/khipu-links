Arithmetic Links Between Khipus

A corpus-wide search for copies, summaries and hierarchies between khipus in the Open Khipu Repository and the Khipu Field Guide database, with every result tested against shuffled-corpus baselines and against relations already known in the literature.

Data: KFG companion data (Khosla & Medrano, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.8125718; 678 khipus), OKR khipu.db (609 khipus) and 19 khipus added to OKR after 2017. Numbers below are knot values as recorded in these databases.

new

1. Berlin: AS149 groups 1 + 2 = AS143 group 4

AS143 = KH0159 = UR1143 (Ethnologisches Museum VA47096, “Ica”) and AS149 = KH0165 = UR1149 (VA44866C in KFG, “Ocucaje”; VA44866 group AS147–149). Both entered the Berlin museum in 1907. Pendant values:

AS143 group 4 = AS149 group 1 + AS149 group 2 17436 = 8653 + 8783 8220 = 4049 + 4171 41883 = 20640 + 21243 1350 = 660 + 690 9528 = 4741 + 4787
  • Combined with the internal sums described by the Aschers for each khipu separately, this gives five levels on two khipus: AS149 part 3 (9 groups) → first group of each subpart of part 2 → AS149 part 1 groups 1 and 2 → AS143 group 4 → AS143 group 1 (sum of groups 2–5).
  • In every position AS149 group 1 is almost exactly half of the combined value (0.496, 0.493, 0.493, 0.489, 0.498).
  • Detector (sumscan.py, cross mode, 0.2% tolerance, only positions where both addends are non-zero and the sum ≥ 100): this is the only cross-khipu “group = group + group” relation in the corpus apart from the three records of KH0049. Shuffled corpora give zero such relations.
  • One allocation system across two khipus. Every group of AS143 and both upper groups of AS149 share the same internal composition over the four main positions, about 22.4% : 10.4% : 54.4% : 12.8% (largest deviation of any group 1.1%, mean 0.5%; among 16 corpus khipus with four or more comparable groups only AS143 itself is as uniform). Measured in ninths of the AS143 total (180,345), the five shares are AS143 group 2 = 0.99, group 3 = 2.05, group 5 = 2.05, AS149 group 1 = 1.93, AS149 group 2 = 1.98: four equal shares and one half share, 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 9. Two of the shares are recorded only on AS149; AS143 keeps their sum as group 4. AS149 then divides its shares further with the same composition (e.g. four identical groups of 1430, 670, 3410, 820). The consistent ratios the Aschers and Chirinos found within each khipu follow from this: one quantity of fixed composition apportioned to five recipients, recorded across two khipus.
  • A third khipu of the same system: AS179 (KH0196 = UR1179, VA47123, Ica, same 1907 batch). Its first group, 1430, 670, 3424, 820 (colours B:BB, B:G, B, G), repeats the four identical subdivision groups of AS149, 1430, 670, 3410, 820 (B:GG, B:GG:LB, B, GG), with the same composition to 0.4%. In the whole corpus (43,940 pendants) the three values 1430, 670 and 820 occur together in a group only on AS149 (four times) and AS179 (once). Found by a composition detector (recipes.py: groups of 4–6 pendants with near-identical profiles on different khipus, cyclic-rotation baseline), which ranks AS149–AS179 first (score 6, baseline 0) and also recovers AS143–AS149. The Aschers’ AS179 entry notes only the batch and a colour pattern. The rest of AS179 (groups of two or three pendants) shows no arithmetic link to AS143 or AS149.

  • Literature check: Ascher & Ascher 1981 (Code of the Quipu, pp. 144–149) treat AS120, AS143 and AS149 together (examples 7.5, 7.6, 5.3) through consistent ratios, and write (p. 146) that there is insufficient data “to either confirm or deny that they are related to one quipumaker”. Their databook entries for AS143, AS147 (comparison of AS147–149), AS149 and AS100 do not state the equality above. Pereyra (1996) sets AS149 aside as a different type. Later work (Urton, Brezine 2005–2011) was only partly checked.
  • The remaining addends of AS143 (groups 2, 3, 5) and the counterpart of AS149 group 2 were not found anywhere in the corpus.
known, detailed

2. Lima → Gothenburg: a copy of one branch

Prior description. Chirinos Rivera (2010, Quipus del Tahuantinsuyo, pp. 233–234, 339–341) already identified UR122 as a partial copy of AS38 (“contiene los mismos valores, en las mismas posiciones”, fewer groups) and used it to argue that colour carries no fixed meaning. What follows adds the group-level mapping, the breakage analysis and the provenance.

Lima: KH0049 = HP036 = AS038 (MNAAHP 3550; AS038 “Museo Nacional 3884-147”), primary cord 31 cm (38.5 cm in AS038), 108 pendants. Aschers’ observation 6: group 1 = group 2 + group 3; group 2 = groups 4 + 5 + 6 (18 pendants each).
Gothenburg: KH0362 = UR122 (Museum of World Culture 1938.45.0228, Nasca, dept. Ica), primary cord 51.5 cm, 72 pendants in four groups of 18. Carlotta record: deposited by Erland Nordenskiöld in 1924 (old no. 1924.06.0117), bought from his estate in 1938, documented by Gary Urton in June 2005.

  • 27 of 31 intact Gothenburg pendants equal their Lima counterparts exactly (e.g. 15652, 6197, 6485, 2967, 2578, 1856). Of the other four, 1510/151 and 1036/136 are the same knots read with or without an empty register; 1605/1615 differs by one ten-knot; 3432/333 is unexplained, and Lima’s internal sum (3432 + 885 = 4317) supports 3432.
  • All other differences follow from breakage: 41 of 72 Gothenburg pendants are broken (termination B). 18 broke above all knots (value 0); 6 lost lower registers (4130→4000, 932→900, 1034→1000, 1016→1000, 3848→3840, 1074→1070); 4 broke inside a knot cluster; on 4 the surviving top knots were recorded one register lower (10643→100, 2263→200, 2711→270, 2166→210).
  • Recording-noise baseline: the two independent recordings of the Lima khipu (AS038, HP036) differ in 8 of the same 72 positions, each by one digit. Gothenburg’s intact cords differ from Lima at about that rate; the case for two different objects rests on the physical evidence (cord length, pendant count, missing groups 1 and 3, intact versus broken cords, Gothenburg accession since 1924/1938).
  • Subsidiaries under equal pendants often carry the same values in a different order, with additions (6197: Lima [10, 17, 47, 149, 177, 4, 1]; Gothenburg [4, 17, 1, 47, 177, 10, 0, 0, 0, 40, 25]).
  • Detector (nearlinks.py, values ≥ 100 within ±2%): 19 near-equal values in one window where the shuffled-corpus maximum is 5; at the aligning offset 26 near-equal pairs, at every other offset at most 5 (mean 0.3). KFG’s “Similar Khipu” navigation also places KH0362 next to KH0049.
  • Colours differ between the recordings (W, W:KB, AB versus AB, MB), which may reflect recorders’ conventions; photographs of both khipus would settle it.

3. Folded copies: separate cord ↔ subsidiary

Incahuasi: UR270 ↔ UR278

UR270 = KH0507 (Proyecto Incahuasi 13830) and UR278 = KH0517 (13833-2014). On 15 consecutive positions (UR270 pendants 18–32 ↔ UR278 pendants 196–210) each UR270 value is a pendant plus one subsidiary, and UR278 carries the sum on a single pendant without subsidiaries.

Waterfall set: UR053B ↔ UR053C

UR053B = KH0284 and UR053C = KH0285 (BCRP ATE 3517). C records triads (AB, W, R); B records pairs (W, R) with one subsidiary on R. Rule: a B record is (W, R, and the AB pendant of the following C triad as R’s subsidiary). Example: C triad 53/W, 13/RL followed by 3/AB ↔ B 53/W, 13+3/RL. On the best alignment 35 fields match (13 W, 11 R, 11 subsidiary); 2,000 shuffles of C’s records give at most 19. Chirinos (KFG blog, 2 Feb 2026) reports that B and C nearly duplicate each other; the packing rule is our addition.

Our order-sensitive copy scans miss both pairs (small values; reordered cords break the diagonal). A record-based detector (records.py: colour period, record totals, shuffled-order baseline) recovers UR270/UR278 on its own (166 bits against a threshold of 90) but scores UR053B/C below threshold (57 bits), because many record totals differ by one.

tested

4. Subsidiaries do not enter pendant sums

For 422 khipus with subsidiaries we counted relations “pendant (≥ 100) = sum of a whole cluster of other pendants” involving at least one cord with subsidiaries, under three readings. Two baselines: values shuffled across positions (cluster boundaries kept, 30 shuffles per khipu), and the stricter offset baseline, counting “pendant = cluster sum + d” for d = ±1…±20.

The folded copies in case 3 show that a subsidiary can be merged into the pendant when an account is rewritten.

5. Huacones-Vilcahuasi: transcription, no links

KH0653–KH0663 (JC024–JC034) exist in OKR only as photographs of handwritten record sheets. We transcribed all eleven (written values checked against the knot descriptions; 2 low-confidence cells). All come from one context: Sector F, unit 8, layer A-1, Elemento 11, lot 97, inventory 782–792, local numbers Quipu 1–11 (Barraza Lescano et al. 2022 report eleven khipus found rolled together in Patio 2 of the North Complex).

A value-shuffling baseline first suggested significance (4 matches against a mean of 0.65, p ≈ 0.013), but shuffling breaks the internal structure of the groups. We withdraw the hierarchy claim; the transcriptions themselves (extracted/huacones_*.csv) remain available.

6. Validations and negative results

Known relations recovered

  • AS069/AS070 (Thompson 2024). A segment-sum detector that is not told where the groups are (segsum.py) splits AS069’s 216 clusters into segments of 20, 10, 20, 21, 24, 24, 25, 23, 24, 25 (Thompson: 18, 10, 22, 21, 23, 25, 25, 24, 24, 25); its first row reproduces Table 7A (63, 28, 56, 24, 56, 29, 24, 18, 0, 5). Error 7.5 against 15.8 for the best of 200 cluster shuffles. In a scan of 2,419 khipu pairs it ranks first, with the other recordings of the same khipu (KH0083, UR035) fourth and fifth.
  • The Paris–Lima copy AS84 (UR1084) ~ HP055 (MNAAHP 29934), mentioned by Chirinos (2010, p. 233), is flagged by our near-copy scan.
  • Duplicate recordings of one object (KH0081 = UR281, KH0083 = UR035 = AS070, KH0049 = HP036 = AS038, KH0001 = UR176 and others), AS114/AS123 (Urton 2005), UR267A/UR255 (Urton & Chu 2019), Puruchuco UR066/UR067.
  • Santa Valley (FitzPatrick, KFG blog 4 Jan 2026). From OKR we get 133 six-cord groups, 63 recto / 69 verso (published 63 / 70), first cords ≈ 1 on recto groups and larger on verso groups. The sum of first cords is 749, not 367 (three large values on KH0326: 203, 102, 105). A detector matching pachaca sizes (18, 23, 9, 7, 41, 32) to runs of groups cut at visible boundaries accepts the real sizes and also 74–100% of random size sets, so the match rests on the total and the moiety split.

Not supported

  • Colonial documents. About 2,300 numbers from roughly 70 open-access transcriptions (Jauja 1570; El quipu colonial 2013 materials; Sisicaya 1588; Arica/Tarapacá 1540–1570; Chachapoyas 1555–1593) against OKR and KFG, by value, by table structure and by household sequence, including regional pairings (Lurín valley, Arica/Lluta, Leymebamba): no match survives the baselines. Three apparent hits were artefacts (round tribute numbers, runs of zeros, runs of single-person households) and disappeared under matched baselines.
  • A sign convention. Neither attachment side nor colour marks negative cords (circular-shift baseline, p = 0.35–0.92).
  • Waterfall E → B sums (Chirinos 2026): the best split of E part IV into four subparts resembles four B/C records, but random sets of E records do about as well (p = 0.07–0.33).
  • Berlin 1907 batch (29 khipus listed with AS100 plus AS147–149): no further addends of AS143 (vector search with one-knot tolerance), and no excess of “total of one khipu = cord of another” over an offset baseline (158 against 141 ± 12). AS149 → AS143 remains the only link found in the batch.
  • No arithmetic links involving the post-2017 OKR khipus (Khipus Enlazados RT-16905, British Museum Am1937,0213.84, SH001, R4303); their values are mostly single digits. Nothing beyond Medrano & Khosla 2024 for UR231 (Monte Sierpe).

7. Two further copies in the Berlin collection

Found by lotmatch.py: whole groups (3–8 pendants, sum ≥ 100, ≥ 3 values ≥ 10) equal on two khipus with different museum numbers, all positions within max(1, 2%) and at least half exactly equal, one extra position allowed. The corpus gives 61 such matches against about 3 on a cyclic-rotation baseline. Most are known duplicate recordings of one object (AS047/KH0058, AS068/UR281, AS125/UR247, AS031/UR044). Two pairs are different objects.

Pachacamac AS175 → Huacho UR233 + UR232

AS175 = UR1175 = KH0192 (VA42518, Pachacamac, 1907 batch): 45 groups of 5 pendants. The Aschers describe three parts: part 1 = groups 1–21, part 2 = groups 22–24, part 3 = groups 25–45; part 2 holds the sums of part 3 (7 sets of 3 groups). Primary cord 210 cm, 114 subsidiaries, median pendant length 64 cm.
UR233 = KH0470 and UR232 = KH0469 (VA63038b, Huacho; KFG primary-cord note on UR232: “Beginning end spliced with UR 233”). Primary cords 66 and 81 cm, 27 subsidiaries in all, median pendant length 41–47 cm.

  • UR233 = part 1 of AS175, group by group (three AS175 groups appear split in two on UR233), UR232 = part 3, 21 groups for 21 groups. Part 2, the summary groups, is not on the Huacho pair.
  • Each Huacho group has one more cord: an empty (knotless) pendant at position 3 on UR233 and position 4 on UR232. On UR232 that cord is always mottled MB:KB, and every position has a fixed colour (W, RB, AB, MB:KB, AB, …), as AS175 has for its five positions (W, LB, YG, YB, YB:0G).
  • Values: 85 of 100 comparable positions equal on UR233, 73 of 105 on UR232. Most differences are one knot (21/22, 100/110, 93/83, 30/32, 50/40). Offsets: matching values ≥ 10 on the aligning diagonal 29 (UR233) and 40 (UR232) against 3.2 ± 4.0 and 2.1 ± 1.9 on all other offsets (z = 6.4 and 19.5).
  • Different objects: pendant counts per group (6 vs 5), lengths, subsidiaries (114 vs 27) and provenance differ. Huacho lies about 180 km north of Pachacamac on the central coast.
  • Prior mention: KBCR lists UR232/UR233 only in an inventory (Urton 2017, p. 261) and in radiocarbon dating (Cherkinsky & Urton 2014, p. 34). We found no description of the link.

Context: for Chucuito in 1567, Chirinos (2010, pp. 88–89, from the Visita de Chucuito) lists distribution khipus (the 1000 pieces of cloth, the 500 mitayos for Potosí, the 18,000 pesos of the tasa) that existed in several copies, each parcialidad declaring in separate interviews what fell to it. A detail copy without the summary part, as on the Huacho pair, is the physical form such a copy could take. Chirinos does not discuss AS175, AS118, UR218 or the Huacho khipus (AS175 appears only in the cover credit, as a replica).

Pachacamac AS118 ← UR218

AS118 = UR1118 = KH0132 (VA42601, 15 pendants) and UR218 = KH0456 (VA42559, 62 pendants), both Pachacamac. Groups 2 and 3 (7 + 7 white pendants) agree cord for cord, including the knot count per cord: [620, 470, 528, 935/932, 474, 319, 453] and [492, 663, 252, 654, 423, 433, 275]. On AS118 five cords of group 3 are broken and keep only their upper registers (600 for 663, 400 for 423, 0 for three), while UR218 has them intact, and cord lengths differ. These are two objects. The first groups differ: AS118 has one pendant with five large subsidiaries [2696, 13182, 14658, 11522, 8565]; UR218 has six coloured pendants.

Methods in brief

  • Cord value = sum of knot_value_type; subtree value adds all subsidiaries. Values are compared on pendants only, on subtrees, or both, as stated per test.
  • Every detector has a baseline built from the corpus itself: values shuffled within each khipu, record order shuffled, cluster order shuffled, annotation shifted circularly, or target values offset by ±d, chosen so that the baseline keeps the structure the test is not about. For sums over groups, value shuffling proved too permissive (it changes the distribution of group totals); the offset baseline is used there. Thresholds are the maximum over shuffled corpora unless a p-value is given.
  • Each detector was run on known cases before being applied to the corpus; the known-case results are in section 6.

Code (Python, standard library) and derived tables, including the Huacones transcriptions, can be shared on request.
