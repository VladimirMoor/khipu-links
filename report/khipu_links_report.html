Independent computational study · open data (OKR, KFG) · September 2026

Arithmetic Links Between Khipus

A corpus-wide search for copies, summaries and hierarchies between khipus in the Open Khipu Repository and the Khipu Field Guide database, with every result tested against shuffled-corpus baselines and against relations already known in the literature.

Data: KFG companion data (Khosla & Medrano, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.8125718; 678 khipus), OKR khipu.db (609 khipus) and 19 khipus added to OKR after 2017. Numbers below are knot values as recorded in these databases.

A 6-page preprint summarising sections 1 and 7: docs/preprint/preprint.pdf in the repository (https://github.com/VladimirMoor/khipu-links).

new

1. Berlin: AS149 groups 1 + 2 = AS143 group 4

AS143 = KH0159 = UR1143 (Ethnologisches Museum VA47096, “Ica”) and AS149 = KH0165 = UR1149 (VA44866C in KFG, “Ocucaje”; VA44866 group AS147–149). Both entered the Berlin museum in 1907. Pendant values:

AS143 group 4 = AS149 group 1 + AS149 group 2 17436 = 8653 + 8783 8220 = 4049 + 4171 41883 = 20640 + 21243 1350 = 660 + 690 9528 = 4741 + 4787
  • Combined with the internal sums described by the Aschers for each khipu separately, this gives five levels on two khipus: AS149 part 3 (9 groups) → first group of each subpart of part 2 → AS149 part 1 groups 1 and 2 → AS143 group 4 → AS143 group 1 (sum of groups 2–5).
  • In every position AS149 group 1 is almost exactly half of the combined value (0.496, 0.493, 0.493, 0.489, 0.498).
  • Detector (sumscan.py, cross mode, 0.2% tolerance, only positions where both addends are non-zero and the sum ≥ 100): this is the only cross-khipu “group = group + group” relation in the corpus apart from the three records of KH0049. Shuffled corpora give zero such relations.
  • One allocation system across the khipus. Every group of AS143 and both upper groups of AS149 share the same internal composition over the four main positions, about 22.4% : 10.4% : 54.4% : 12.8% (largest deviation of any group 1.1%, mean 0.5%; among 16 corpus khipus with four or more comparable groups only AS143 itself is as uniform). Measured in ninths of the AS143 total (180,345), the five shares are AS143 group 2 = 0.99, group 3 = 2.05, group 5 = 2.05, AS149 group 1 = 1.93, AS149 group 2 = 1.98: four equal shares and one half share, 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 9. Two of the shares are recorded only on AS149; AS143 keeps their sum as group 4. AS149 then divides its shares further with the same composition (e.g. four identical groups of 1430, 670, 3410, 820). The consistent ratios the Aschers and Chirinos found within each khipu follow from this: one quantity of fixed composition apportioned to five recipients, recorded across two khipus.
  • A third khipu of the same system: AS179 (KH0196 = UR1179, VA47123, Ica, same 1907 batch). Its first group, 1430, 670, 3424, 820 (colours B:BB, B:G, B, G), repeats the four identical subdivision groups of AS149, 1430, 670, 3410, 820 (B:GG, B:GG:LB, B, GG), with the same composition to 0.4%. In the whole corpus (43,940 pendants) the three values 1430, 670 and 820 occur together in a group only on AS149 (four times) and AS179 (once). Found by a composition detector (recipes.py: groups of 4–6 pendants with near-identical profiles on different khipus, cyclic-rotation baseline), which ranks AS149–AS179 first (score 6, baseline 0) and also recovers AS143–AS149. The Aschers’ AS179 entry notes only the batch and a colour pattern. The rest of AS179 (groups of two or three pendants) shows no arithmetic link to AS143 or AS149.
  • A possible second breakdown of the same total on AS179 (suggestive, p ≈ 0.06). Position 1 of AS149 share 1 is 8,653, which AS149 itself breaks down over its seven lots (1510 + 4 × 1430 + 1320 + 103 = 8653). The four pendants of AS179 in colour B:BB, taken from four different groups, sum to the same total: 1430 + 2740 + 3450 + 1033 = 8653 (coloursum.py). AS179 has only three colour totals above 100 that are not round (1112, 5091, 8653); shifting the AS149 share values by ±1…±200 produces a match in 1 of 400 shifts. However, this match was found in a corpus-wide scan, and over all six directed pairs of the three khipus (all cords and group sums as targets, colour totals of at least two pendants) one match is expected by chance with probability about 0.06 (coloursum.py). No other colour total of AS179 matches a share position. We therefore treat it as a suggestive coincidence, not as an established link.
  • Two equal shares with a substitution. AS143 groups 3 and 5, both 2/9 shares, total 41,062 and 41,061 and have the same first position (9094). The difference is a shift within the share: −54 in position 2, −320 in position 3, +373 in position 4 (sum −1). Given the spread of the four 2/9 shares (SD ≈ 950), totals equal within 1 arise with probability about 0.001 (about 0.005 allowing for the six pairs we could have noticed); neither the Aschers nor Chirinos comment on it. It reads as two shares of the same size, one with about 374 units of goods 2 and 3 replaced by good 4 — the same kind of reallocation Chirinos describes on AS120. Across the whole corpus, pairs of same-length groups with totals equal within 1 are not over-represented (12 pairs vs 7.8 expected from nearby differences, p ≈ 0.1; equalshares.py), so this is a feature of this khipu, not a general convention.
  • Unique in the corpus. A scan of every group of every khipu against position-wise sums of 2–10 consecutive groups, or of 2–8 groups taken at a stride of 2–4, of every other khipu (vecsum.py, ≥ 3 values ≥ 10) finds exactly one exact equality: this one. With the target values shifted by 1–7, the scan finds 0–2, all on small values.

Their databook entries for AS143, AS147 (comparison of AS147–149), AS149 and AS100 do not state the equality above. Pereyra (1996) sets AS149 aside as a different type. Later work (Urton, Brezine 2005–2011) was only partly checked.
  • The remaining addends of AS143 (groups 2, 3, 5) and the counterpart of AS149 group 2 were not found anywhere in the corpus.

2. Lima → Gothenburg: a copy of one branch

Prior description. Chirinos Rivera (2010, Quipus del Tahuantinsuyo, pp. 233–234, 339–341) already identified UR122 as a partial copy of AS38 (“contiene los mismos valores, en las mismas posiciones”, fewer groups) and used it to argue that colour carries no fixed meaning. What follows adds the group-level mapping, the breakage analysis and the provenance.

Lima: KH0049 = HP036 = AS038 (MNAAHP 3550; AS038 “Museo Nacional 3884-147”), primary cord 31 cm (38.5 cm in AS038), 108 pendants. Aschers’ observation 6: group 1 = group 2 + group 3; group 2 = groups 4 + 5 + 6 (18 pendants each).
Gothenburg: KH0362 = UR122 (Museum of World Culture 1938.45.0228, Nasca, dept. Ica), primary cord 51.5 cm, 72 pendants in four groups of 18. Carlotta record: deposited by Erland Nordenskiöld in 1924 (old no. 1924.06.0117), bought from his estate in 1938, documented by Gary Urton in June 2005.

  • 27 of 31 intact Gothenburg pendants equal their Lima counterparts exactly (e.g. 15652, 6197, 6485, 2967, 2578, 1856). Of the other four, 1510/151 and 1036/136 are the same knots read with or without an empty register; 1605/1615 differs by one ten-knot; 3432/333 is unexplained, and Lima’s internal sum (3432 + 885 = 4317) supports 3432.
  • All other differences follow from breakage: 41 of 72 Gothenburg pendants are broken (termination B). 18 broke above all knots (value 0); 6 lost lower registers (4130→4000, 932→900, 1034→1000, 1016→1000, 3848→3840, 1074→1070); 4 broke inside a knot cluster; on 4 the surviving top knots were recorded one register lower (10643→100, 2263→200, 2711→270, 2166→210).

Gothenburg’s intact cords differ from Lima at about that rate; the case for two different objects rests on the physical evidence (cord length, pendant count, missing groups 1 and 3, intact versus broken cords, Gothenburg accession since 1924/1938).
  • Subsidiaries under equal pendants often carry the same values in a different order, with additions (6197: Lima [10, 17, 47, 149, 177, 4, 1]; Gothenburg [4, 17, 1, 47, 177, 10, 0, 0, 0, 40, 25]).
  • Detector (nearlinks.py, values ≥ 100 within ±2%): 19 near-equal values in one window where the shuffled-corpus maximum is 5; at the aligning offset 26 near-equal pairs, at every other offset at most 5 (mean 0.3). KFG’s “Similar Khipu” navigation also places KH0362 next to KH0049.
  • Colours differ between the recordings (W, W:KB, AB versus AB, MB), which may reflect recorders’ conventions; photographs of both khipus would settle it.

Incahuasi: UR270 ↔ UR278

UR270 = KH0507 (Proyecto Incahuasi 13830) and UR278 = KH0517 (13833-2014). On 15 consecutive positions (UR270 pendants 18–32 ↔ UR278 pendants 196–210) each UR270 value is a pendant plus one subsidiary, and UR278 carries the sum on a single pendant without subsidiaries.

Waterfall set: UR053B ↔ UR053C

UR053B = KH0284 and UR053C = KH0285 (BCRP ATE 3517). C records triads (AB, W, R); B records pairs (W, R) with one subsidiary on R. Rule: a B record is (W, R, and the AB pendant of the following C triad as R’s subsidiary). Example: C triad 53/W, 13/RL followed by 3/AB ↔ B 53/W, 13+3/RL. On the best alignment 35 fields match (13 W, 11 R, 11 subsidiary); 2,000 shuffles of C’s records give at most 19. Chirinos (KFG blog, 2 Feb 2026) reports that B and C nearly duplicate each other; the packing rule is our addition.

Knot direction. Hyland (2014, 2015) reads S and Z knots as moiety markers (upper/lower) on the Mangas khipu board. In our copy pairs both copies share the archive's usual direction (Inkawasi pairs 5–17% S knots on both sides; Huacho 16–17%). The one exception is the Waterfall set: UR053A, B, D and E are 99–100% S, while UR053C is tied in blocks, S on cords 3–13, Z on cords 14–41, S again on 42–61. UR053B copies C from cord 11 to the end, so it contains the whole Z block, all in S knots: here knot direction was not carried into the copy.

Our order-sensitive copy scans miss both pairs (small values; reordered cords break the diagonal). A record-based detector (records.py: colour period, record totals, shuffled-order baseline) recovers UR270/UR278 on its own (166 bits against a threshold of 90) but scores UR053B/C below threshold (57 bits), because many record totals differ by one.

4. Subsidiaries do not enter pendant sums

For 422 khipus with subsidiaries we counted relations “pendant (≥ 100) = sum of a whole cluster of other pendants” involving at least one cord with subsidiaries, under three readings. Two baselines: values shuffled across positions (cluster boundaries kept, 30 shuffles per khipu), and the stricter offset baseline, counting “pendant = cluster sum + d” for d = ±1…±20.

Consistent with KH0049 (sums hold on pendant values; the Aschers found subsidiaries summing separately, colour by colour) and with K-CAT phase 10 (parent = sum of children in only 6.6% of groups). The folded copies in case 3 show that a subsidiary can be merged into the pendant when an account is rewritten.

KH0653–KH0663 (JC024–JC034) exist in OKR only as photographs of handwritten record sheets. We transcribed all eleven (written values checked against the knot descriptions; 2 low-confidence cells). All come from one context: Sector F, unit 8, layer A-1, Elemento 11, lot 97, inventory 782–792, local numbers Quipu 1–11 (Barraza Lescano et al. 2022 report eleven khipus found rolled together in Patio 2 of the North Complex).

A value-shuffling baseline first suggested significance (4 matches against a mean of 0.65, p ≈ 0.013), but shuffling breaks the internal structure of the groups. With an offset baseline, which keeps all structure and counts “total = cord + d” for d = ±1…±15, the same four matches sit at the level of chance (mean 2.6, sd 1.25, z = 1.1). We withdraw the hierarchy claim; the transcriptions themselves (extracted/huacones_*.csv) remain available.

Check against the publication. Table 3 of Barraza Lescano et al. (2022) gives, for each khipu, the numbers of pendants, subsidiaries, knots (knot clusters), top cords and knots on top cords. Our transcription agrees on all five numbers for 9 of the 11 khipus (one cluster off on JC032; on JC028 when its separate verso pair, cords 1–2, is counted as top cords) (huacones_table3.py). The two remaining rows are crossed. JC024 (sheet “Quipu 8”, inventory 789) matches row 4 exactly (7 pendants, 7 subsidiaries, 14 knots, 1 top cord with 3). JC027 (sheet “Quipu 4”, inventory 785) matches row 8 in total cords (12 = 9 + 3) and knots (24). Since the inventory numbers 782–792 follow the sheet numbers, the published rows 4 and 8 are probably swapped. Three of the twelve cords we read as pendants on JC027 are then subsidiaries.

6. Validations and negative results

Known relations recovered

  • AS069/AS070 (Thompson 2024). A segment-sum detector that is not told where the groups are (segsum.py) splits AS069’s 216 clusters into segments of 20, 10, 20, 21, 24, 24, 25, 23, 24, 25 (Thompson: 18, 10, 22, 21, 23, 25, 25, 24, 24, 25); its first row reproduces Table 7A (63, 28, 56, 24, 56, 29, 24, 18, 0, 5). Error 7.5 against 15.8 for the best of 200 cluster shuffles. In a scan of 2,419 khipu pairs it ranks first, with the other recordings of the same khipu (KH0083, UR035) fourth and fifth.
  • The Paris–Lima copy AS84 (UR1084) ~ HP055 (MNAAHP 29934), mentioned by Chirinos (2010, p. 233), is flagged by our near-copy scan.
  • Duplicate recordings of one object (AS208 = UR083 and UR1107 = UR237, both read from opposite ends; AS046 = HP041; KH0081 = UR281, KH0083 = UR035 = AS070, KH0049 = HP036 = AS038, KH0001 = UR176 and others), AS114/AS123 (Urton 2005), UR267A/UR255 (Urton & Chu 2019), Puruchuco UR066/UR067.
  • Top cords (Ascher; Chirinos 2010, ch. 15). In the 17 khipus with top cords, 12 have at least one top cord equal to the sum of the neighbouring pendant group; 21 of 63 non-zero top cords match, against about 1 on an offset baseline (±1…±10). Every match sums the group before the top cord, none the group after (possibly a recording convention; see section 7, AS003). Groups of six pendants make up 24% of groups on these khipus and 8% elsewhere (topcords.py).
  • Broken cords (termination B) as lower bounds. In groups where the first or last cord is within 3% of the sum of the others (value ≥ 30), the sum is exact in 68% of groups with no broken part but in 35% when a part is broken (Fisher p ≈ 0.0003). The drop holds outside Inkawasi (1 of 14 against 11 of 25; p ≈ 0.03) and at Inkawasi (21 of 51 against 53 of 72; p ≈ 0.0004). The sign of the discrepancy, however, does not favour lost knots: parts fall short of the total in 22 of 33 inexact groups with a broken part and in 19 of 27 with none (p ≈ 0.8). So a value on a broken cord should be treated as uncertain, not simply as too small (broken.py). This agrees with the AS118/AS125 case (section 7), where the one correction needed on a broken cord (2220 → 2298) goes upward, but it does not prove it.
  • Santa Valley (FitzPatrick, KFG blog 4 Jan 2026). From OKR we get 133 six-cord groups, 63 recto / 69 verso (published 63 / 70), first cords ≈ 1 on recto groups and larger on verso groups. The sum of first cords is 749, not 367 (three large values on KH0326: 203, 102, 105). A detector matching pachaca sizes (18, 23, 9, 7, 41, 32) to runs of groups cut at visible boundaries accepts the real sizes and also 74–100% of random size sets, so the match rests on the total and the moiety split.

New data. Two Dumbarton Oaks khipus given by Bill Conklin, UR297 (PC.WBC.2016.072; 438 pendants, 623 cords with subsidiaries) and UR298 (PC.WBC.2016.071; 203 pendants, 285 cords), are in the OKR only as scans of Urton's handwritten record sheets (data/KH0674_0675). We transcribed all 65 pages (extracted/do_UR29*.csv, do_ingest.py). Urton's own notes on the last eight pages give the total of each group of UR297 and of its summary cord; 74 of his 78 totals equal ours (do_check.py). UR297 is a khipu of 41 groups, mostly ten cords plus one summary cord pulled up like a top cord, which Urton had already noted. Neither khipu shares runs, groups or summaries with any other khipu of the corpus beyond chance (excerpts 189 as before; summaries 389 against 419.5 on the offset baseline).

Not supported

  • Colonial documents. About 2,300 numbers from roughly 70 open-access transcriptions (Jauja 1570; El quipu colonial 2013 materials; Sisicaya 1588; Arica/Tarapacá 1540–1570; Chachapoyas 1555–1593) against OKR and KFG, by value, by table structure and by household sequence, including regional pairings (Lurín valley, Arica/Lluta, Leymebamba): no match survives the baselines. Three apparent hits were artefacts (round tribute numbers, runs of zeros, runs of single-person households) and disappeared under matched baselines.
  • Opposite attachment in copies. Urton and Chu (2015) note that the matching Inkawasi pairs are attached recto on one khipu and verso on the other. Across ten pairs of copies of different objects (Inkawasi, Waterfall, MM015/UR212, Paris–Lima, Puruchuco, Lima–Gothenburg) the sides are opposite in 7, against 50% for random pairs (p ≈ 0.17; attach_copies.py). The Inkawasi pattern is not a general rule; the same-side cases are UR273B/UR274B, UR066/UR067 and KH0049/UR122.
  • Same suppliers in the same order. Urton and Chu (2019) read the 24–27 deposits per khipu as 24–27 shipment points. If the points delivered in a fixed order, deposit sizes should correlate by position between khipus of different products. Over 36 pairs of Inkawasi deposit lists (UR255, UR256, UR266, UR267A/B, UR268, UR274A/B, UR275) only the copy UR267A/UR255 correlates clearly (ρ = 0.67); the smallest other p is 0.013, below the level expected from 36 tests.
  • A sign convention. Neither attachment side nor colour marks negative cords (circular-shift baseline, p = 0.35–0.92).
  • Waterfall E → B sums (Chirinos 2026): the best split of E part IV into four subparts resembles four B/C records, but random sets of E records do about as well (p = 0.07–0.33).
  • Berlin 1907 batch (29 khipus listed with AS100 plus AS147–149): no further addends of AS143 (vector search with one-knot tolerance), and no excess of “total of one khipu = cord of another” over an offset baseline (158 against 141 ± 12). AS149 → AS143 remains the only link found in the batch.
  • No arithmetic links involving the post-2017 OKR khipus (Khipus Enlazados RT-16905, British Museum Am1937,0213.84, SH001, R4303); their values are mostly single digits. Nothing beyond Medrano & Khosla 2024 for UR231 (Monte Sierpe).

7. Two further copies in the Berlin collection

Found by lotmatch.py: whole groups (3–8 pendants, sum ≥ 100, ≥ 3 values ≥ 10) equal on two khipus with different museum numbers, all positions within max(1, 2%) and at least half exactly equal, one extra position allowed. The corpus gives 61 such matches against about 3 on a cyclic-rotation baseline. Most are known duplicate recordings of one object (AS047/KH0058, AS068/UR281, AS125/UR247, AS031/UR044). Two pairs are different objects.

Pachacamac AS175 → Huacho UR233 + UR232

AS175 = UR1175 = KH0192 (VA42518, Pachacamac, 1907 batch): 45 groups of 5 pendants. The Aschers describe three parts: part 1 = groups 1–21, part 2 = groups 22–24, part 3 = groups 25–45; part 2 holds the sums of part 3 (7 sets of 3 groups). Primary cord 210 cm, 114 subsidiaries, median pendant length 64 cm.
UR233 = KH0470 and UR232 = KH0469 (VA63038b, Huacho; KFG primary-cord note on UR232: “Beginning end spliced with UR 233”). Primary cords 66 and 81 cm, 27 subsidiaries in all, median pendant length 41–47 cm.

  • UR233 = part 1 of AS175, group by group (three AS175 groups appear split in two on UR233), UR232 = part 3, 21 groups for 21 groups. Part 2, the summary groups, is not on the Huacho pair.
  • Each Huacho group has one more cord, without knots: position 3 on UR233 and position 4 on UR232. On UR233 these cords are intact (16 of 18 knotted ends, median 46 cm) and genuinely empty. On UR232 the extra cord is always dark MB:KB and 18 of 20 are broken (median 10.5 cm left, against 53 cm for the other cords), so whatever it carried is lost. Every UR232 position has a fixed colour (W, RB, AB, MB:KB, AB, …), as AS175 has for its five positions (W, LB, YG, YB, YB:0G).
  • Subsidiaries are copied too, in a different colour. AS175 has a white (W) subsidiary on its white first pendant in nearly every group. On the Huacho pair the first pendant carries a black (KB, once RL) subsidiary instead. The five that are intact equal AS175's subsidiary at the same place (25, 6, 7, 6, 0); the other 22 are black stubs of 0.5–2.5 cm with broken ends, so their values are lost. The subsidiaries of AS175's second (LB) pendants, 10–72 each, have no counterpart on the Huacho pair.
  • The Huacho copy fits AS175's own sums better than AS175 does. Part 2 of AS175 (groups 22–24) holds the sums of the first, second and third groups of the seven sets of part 3. Summing part 3 as recorded on AS175 reproduces 5 of the 15 part-2 values exactly; summing the same groups as recorded on UR232 reproduces 8. Taking each of the seven cords from either version, 10 of 15 totals can be met exactly (target shifted by ±1…±20: 1.5 on average, at most 5). (The Aschers, Databook p. 1074, count 8 of the 15 as exact “or off by 1 in 1 digit” on AS175 and 5 more as sums of some of the 7 pendants; with the same one-knot tolerance we get 6 on AS175 and 10 on UR232.) Five totals need Huacho values where the versions differ: 335 needs 18 (UR232) instead of 108 (AS175); 268 needs 32 instead of 30; 108 needs 3 and 7 instead of 5 and 6; the whole second column in groups 22 and 23 (35, 36) matches only the UR232 values. One total (192) needs the AS175 values; five match neither. The Huacho copy seems to preserve the values from which the sums were made; whether AS175's differing values are later changes or reading errors would need the objects themselves.
  • Values: 85 of 100 comparable positions equal on UR233, 73 of 105 on UR232. Most differences are one knot (21/22, 100/110, 93/83, 30/32, 50/40). Offsets: matching values ≥ 10 on the aligning diagonal 29 (UR233) and 40 (UR232) against 3.2 ± 4.0 and 2.1 ± 1.9 on all other offsets (z = 6.4 and 19.5).
  • Different objects: pendant counts per group (6 vs 5), lengths, subsidiaries (114 vs 27) and provenance differ. Huacho lies about 180 km north of Pachacamac on the central coast.
  • Prior mention: KBCR lists UR232/UR233 only in an inventory (Urton 2017, p. 261) and in radiocarbon dating (Cherkinsky & Urton 2014, p. 34). We found no description of the link.

Context: for Chucuito in 1567, Chirinos (2010, pp. 88–89, from the Visita de Chucuito) lists distribution khipus (the 1000 pieces of cloth, the 500 mitayos for Potosí, the 18,000 pesos of the tasa) that existed in several copies, each parcialidad declaring in separate interviews what fell to it. A detail copy without the summary part, as on the Huacho pair, is the physical form such a copy could take. Chirinos does not discuss AS175, AS118, UR218 or the Huacho khipus (AS175 appears only in the cover credit, as a replica).

Empty slots are not peculiar to the Huacho copy (blanks.py). Among 154 khipus with regular groups (≥ 5 groups of equal length ≥ 3), 11 have a position where the pendant is intact (not broken, at least half the median length) but knotless in ≥ 80% of the groups; shuffling values within groups gives 3.0 (at most 6 in 200 shuffles). Five more khipus have regular groups and no knots at all (AS067, AS071, HP025, QU001, UR070). The category layout of a record seems to have been fixed before, and independently of, the numbers entered in it.

A slot empty on one copy and filled on the other (fillslot.py). In the Inkawasi pair UR255 ~ UR267A, matched cord for cord by Urton and Chu (2015, 2019), each group holds a gross value, the fixed 15 and a net value (gross = 15 + net), in the order [gross, net, 15] on UR255 and [gross, 15, net] on UR267A. Of 19 groups that can be paired by their gross value and have a net value on UR267A, UR255 leaves the net pendant empty in the first 12 (11 of them intact, knotted ends, 34–45 cm) and fills it with the same value in 5 of the last 6. A clean split like this is unlikely if empty slots were scattered at random (about 1 in 3,000). The khipu tied to UR255, UR256 (fixed value 10), shows the same order: where a group has the layout [gross, slot, 10, …], the slot is empty in the first 6 such groups (5 intact) and filled in the next 12 (gross = net + 10 in 7 of them), a split with probability about 1 in 18,000 under random placement. On UR256 the early groups also carry itemised values after the 10, so there the empty slot may belong to a different kind of entry. A second Inkawasi pair shows the same pattern more starkly. UR273B and UR274B (both Sector A, unit 20N, each tied to another khipu: UR273A, UR274A) share six deposit totals (1200, 1249, 1332, 1575, 2142, 2300; 0.45 expected when values are offset, Poisson p ≈ 1e-5). UR273B records mostly the total only, with the other pendants of the group empty and often intact (e.g. [0, 1249, 0, 0, 0, 0], [1575, 0, 0]); UR274B records the same deposits in full, with the deduction 10 or 17 and a breakdown ([1575, 1000, 17], [2300, 0, 10, 472]); a block of UR273B groups in the middle (groups 6–12) already carries the deduction and breakdown, so the filling is by section rather than “empty first, filled later” as on UR255. Across the archive, copies are found in a different bundle from their counterpart (UR255 + UR256 ↔ UR267A + UR267B; UR273A + UR273B ↔ UR274A + UR274B), and the three peanut khipus with the deduction 47 (UR266, UR275; on UR269 in the first place of each block) share deposits. Urton and Chu (2015) do not discuss empty slots in the text; their inventory (Appendix I) notes blanks on UR273A and UR273B and relates UR273B to UR266 and UR275, whereas in the OKR values UR273B shares its totals with UR274B (6, against 0.5 expected) and with neither UR266 nor UR275 (0 each).

Urton (2014, Quipus de Pachacamac, p. 15) lists six pairs of “similar” Pachacamac khipus with nearly the same number of cords and colour pattern (UR1131/UR1095, UR1144/UR218, UR1151/45189, UR1096/UR196, HP018/UR243, UR226/UR230) and reads them as a double-entry check between those who brought goods and the accountants of the site. In the five pairs present in our data the cord values agree at chance level (1–3 equal values at the best offset, against 0–3 at other offsets). The numerical copies above (AS175 → UR233/UR232, UR218 → AS118) are not in his list; UR218's structural twin (UR1144) and its numerical copy (AS118) are different khipus.

Product labels at Inkawasi. The OKR records give excavators' nicknames and find units: “Khipu del Ají” (UR267A/B), “Khipu del Maní” (UR266, UR268, UR269, UR270, all Unidad 20N) and “Khipu de frijoles” (UR271). The fixed deduction follows the product: 15 and 10 on the chili khipus and on their copies UR255/UR256 (Sector C, “big pile”); 47 only on khipus of the peanut unit 20N (UR266, UR269, UR275). A needlework bundle with a red tassel at the start of the primary cord occurs on two khipus of the archive, UR266 and UR268, both peanut khipus (2 of 4 against 0 of 25; p ≈ 0.015). Colour does not mark the product (see above). The product is thus labelled by a number (the deduction) and possibly by a physical marker, not by a knot sign. Urton and Chu (2019) relate the fixed values to the produce under which the khipus lay; the tassel observation is ours.

A deduction that depends on the place of the record. UR269 (“Khipu del Maní”, 20N) is laid out in blocks of about five three-cord records (gross, net, deduction) separated by single empty cords. Unlike the other Inkawasi khipus, whose deduction is one constant (47, 15, 10, 17…), UR269’s deduction follows the place of the record in its block: 47 in the first, 46 in the second and third, 44 in the fourth, 25 in the fifth. 33 of 43 readable deductions equal the value of their place, against 17 when records are shuffled within blocks (p ≈ 0.0005; slotded.py). The deduction is not a percentage: records in places 2–4 are the largest (about 2,100–2,800) and those in place 5 the smallest (about 400–830). Each block therefore reads as one round of five deliveries in a fixed order, each with its own fixed deduction, the “constant order of goods” Chirinos describes in colonial accounts. The other Inkawasi khipus show no such dependence (all p > 0.8). Colour does not mark the place: in all five places the gross cord is white and the net cord dark or light brown, and the deduction cords mix the same browns. Urton and Chu (2015, p. 522; Chu and Urton 2018) list UR269 among the peanut-room khipus with no repeating value, and their 2019 study of the deductions registers only the fixed values 10, 15, 47 and 208 (with 17 and 30 as inconsistent cases), so this structure is not described there. It also bears on their conclusion that the deduction was about 2% of the average deposit (UR275, UR268, UR267B): on UR269 the ratio is 1.9% in places 2–4 but 3.0% in place 1 and 4.4% in place 5, so within one khipu the deduction follows the place, not the size of the deposit. Our counts reproduce their deposit sizes approximately (UR275: 24 deposits with 47, mean 2,229, against their 25 and 2,257.8). Their supplementary table of cord values for UR267A, UR267B, UR275 and UR268 contains every OKR value of these khipus, subsidiaries included, in the same order; the one difference is the first fixed value on UR268, 20[8] in their table and 200 in OKR (sup2019.py). A numerical coincidence worth testing: the five place deductions of UR269 add up to 47 + 46 + 46 + 44 + 25 = 208, the fixed deduction of UR268, the other peanut khipu from the same room. A complete UR269 block totals 7,500–10,400 (mean about 9,100), close to the UR268 deposits (mean 10,243 according to Urton and Chu), so one UR268 deposit could be one round of five UR269 deliveries. No UR269 block total equals a UR268 deposit, however (no shared values beyond chance: 4 against 1.35), and no UR268 deposit, gross or net of 208, is the sum of 4–6 consecutive UR269 records more often than chance (exactly: 3, all across block boundaries or with blank cords, against 1.6 on an offset baseline; within 0.5%: 50 against 49.8; ur268_269.py). The equality 208 = 47 + 46 + 46 + 44 + 25 therefore stays an arithmetic coincidence without a record-level link.

Pachacamac AS118: a khipu that links two others

AS118 = UR1118 = KH0132 (VA42601) has three groups. The Aschers (Databook, pp. 791–793) note that cord 1 “is not a regular pendant attachment”: its twisted end is linked through the strands of the main cord, so it was attached before being twisted and knotted; and that its subsidiaries (2696–14658) differ in magnitude from the pendants (275–935). They do not connect it to another khipu. KFG/Ascher note: group 2 “is actually on a ‘subsidiary’ main cord” (Urton), i.e. a separately attached piece.

  • Groups 2–3 copy UR218 (KH0456, VA42559, 62 pendants): 7 + 7 white pendants cord for cord, including the knot count per cord: [620, 470, 528, 935/932, 474, 319, 453] and [492, 663, 252, 654, 423, 433, 275]. On AS118 five cords of group 3 are broken and keep only their upper registers (600 for 663, 400 for 423, 0 for three); UR218 has them intact, and cord lengths differ, so these are two objects.
  • Group 1 carries the group totals of AS125 (KH0140 = UR247, VA42670). It is a single empty pendant (W:RG) with five subsidiaries [2696, 13182, 14658, 11522, 8565]. Subsidiaries 2–5 follow AS125's group sums in order, [13676, 14658, 11444, 8584]: 14658 exactly; 8584 would be 8565 if the first cord of group 4 is 3111: the Aschers' own record gives no value for it (“?”), only knots 3s(10.0); 1s(18.0); 1s(26.0); 1s(37.0); 1s(39.0), i.e. 3 thousands, 1 hundred, 1 ten and two single knots in the units zone (KFG has 3130, Urton 31111; Chirinos 2010, p. 244, tabulates 3112, which gives 8566, one more than the subsidiary); 11444 would be 11522 if the first cord of group 3 was 2298: the Aschers record it as “222?”, knots 2s(9.5); 2s(18.0); 2s(27.5), broken at 29.5 cm just below the tens, so its units are lost (Chirinos tabulates it as 2222, which gives 11446); 13676 against 13182 is unexplained. AS125 group 5 is a single broken YB cord without knots.
  • Against the group-sum chains of all 696 other khipus (best offset, 1% tolerance), only AS125 matches more than one of the five values (3; its second recording UR247 gives 2); with the values shifted by ±100…±300, no khipu matches three.
  • All three khipus are from the Berlin Pachacamac batch. We found no description of either link (KBCR lists them together only in inventories: Urton 2014, pp. 43–47; Urton 2017, pp. 123–124).

Basel IVc.366.03 (Huacho): fragments repeating two Berlin Pachacamac khipus

Object IVc.366.03 of the Museum der Kulturen Basel is a black cloth, 95.5 × 477 cm, onto which ten fragmentary khipus and hundreds of loose cords were sewn in 1924 under Erland Nordenskiöld. According to the museum’s 1910 annual report, as found by Medrano (2022, pp. 124–127), the cords were dug from a burial ground near Huacho by the ship’s doctor Arnold Masarey and bought from him in 1910. The OKR records MM008–MM017 are the ten fragments; their provenance field (“Eduard Gaffron”) does not agree with this archive. Six of the ten fragments repeat runs of cords from two khipus of the Berlin Pachacamac batch: UR212 (VA42508(A), 195 pendants, recorded by Urton) and UR1131 = AS131 (VA42510, 146 pendants, recorded by the Aschers) (basel_huacho.py).

  • Not chance. The fragments match 37 of UR212’s 195 pendants and 25 of UR1131’s 146. Against each of the 121 other khipus with 100 or more pendants they match at most 4 cords (median 0).
  • Direction. Within every fragment that carries both, the UR212 runs and the UR1131 runs go in opposite directions (MM008, MM015, MM016). The fragments as a whole face both ways (MM008 against MM015 and MM016), as expected of pieces sewn onto a cloth. The two Berlin records were made by different investigators, who may have read the khipus from opposite ends. The reversal therefore says nothing about how the khipus were made. This corrects our earlier statement that it was “in the cords themselves”, which wrongly assumed that both Berlin records were Urton’s.
  • Two halves of one account, joined at Huacho. UR212 and AS131 each have 14 main groups: 14 cords per group on UR212 (plus two short groups), 10 on AS131 (plus four short groups of 1–2 cords). Number UR212's main groups from its end and AS131's from its start. Then each of the three fragments that carries both khipus pairs the same unit: MM015 unit 1 (all 14 cords of UR212's unit, then 9 of AS131's 10), MM016 unit 4 (UR212's full unit, then the start of AS131's), MM008 unit 14 (AS131's full unit, then UR212's, with one extra cord of 113). By chance the second and third pairings would agree with probability (1/14)² ≈ 0.005. The rule that assigns a run to the unit holding most of its knotted cords was fixed after we saw that one knotless boundary cord of MM016 falls in the next unit (ur212_as131_units.py). The unit totals of UR212 and AS131 are not correlated (Spearman ρ = 0.19, p ≈ 0.27), so the two Berlin khipus hold different categories for the same 14 units. The Huacho original appears to have put them side by side, 14 + 10 = 24 cords per unit. This mirrors the Pachacamac AS175 → Huacho UR233 + UR232 case in reverse: there, one Pachacamac account is split over two Huacho khipus; here, two Pachacamac khipus are joined on one Huacho khipu.
  • The same two parts of seven. The Aschers (Databook pp. 852, 866) divide AS131 into two parts of seven groups, separated by a larger space on the primary cord. UR212 has the same division: a single knotless cord (its group 9) separates its main groups into 7 + 7, and counted from UR212's end it falls between units 7 and 8, exactly where AS131's space falls (halves_columns.py). This is structural evidence for the unit pairing that does not depend on the Basel fragments. Of the Aschers' three equalities within AS131, the OKR values reproduce one exactly (233 = 233) and two to within 1 and 5. Between the two khipus, three sums of a position over a part are equal (61, 65, 178), against 0.4 with offset values (maximum 2 in 20 offsets). Two of the three involve AS131's marked seventh cord. No equality holds unit by unit, so this is suggestive only.
  • A 14-unit layout in Gretzer's batch (suggestive). Fourteen main groups (≥ 5 cords) are rare in the corpus: 5 of 308 khipus outside the Berlin “Pachacamac” batch (1.6%). Within that batch, 4 of 23 have them: UR212 (14 cords per unit), AS131 (10), AS170 (VA42554, 5–7) and UR199 (VA42597(A), 5). The binomial P ≈ 0.0005, but P ≈ 0.04 once the two khipus that prompted the question are left out (units14.py). The Berlin records for Gretzer khipus that have not been digitized describe three more: VA42513 (“14 × 13 pendants”), VA42537 (“14 × A, A = 9 pendants”) and VA42532 (7 × A, 7 × B, 7 × A, 7 × B, i.e. two halves of 7 + 7). So the batch may hold several records of the same 14 units, each with a different set of categories. The unit totals of the four digitized ones are not correlated (one of 12 pairwise tests at p = 0.02).
  • Marked slots empty in Berlin, filled at Huacho (suggestive). Each group of AS131 has a dark cord in the seventh place (NB:KB), and each group of UR212 a cord of a different colour in the third or fourth place (AB:CB). Across the five units the fragments carry in full, a knotless cord in Berlin is filled on the Basel copy only at these marked places: AS131 unit 1 (0 → 4, W:GL), AS131 unit 14 (0 → 14, W:PB) and UR212 unit 14 (0 → 22, GL:MB). Where the marked cord has a value in Berlin (UR212 unit 1, 25), Basel has the same value (B:GL). The other knotless cords stay knotless (2 of 2); 41 other values are equal and 5 differ, all in unit 14. With 3 of 5 Berlin zeros filled, the chance that they are exactly the three marked ones is 1 in 10, so this is suggestive only (basel_slots.py). It parallels the Inkawasi pair UR255 ~ UR267A, where a slot empty on one copy is filled on the other (section 7).
  • Copies, not the same cords. The Basel cords are in Basel and the Berlin cords are in Berlin, and the values and subsidiaries differ in places: 103 against 102; 4 against a dark knotless cord; 84 and 6 swapped; subsidiaries on MM015’s 212 but not on UR212’s, and none on MM015’s run from UR1131, where UR1131 has one on almost every pendant. The Huacho cords are thus a second record of parts of two Pachacamac accounts. The same holds for our earlier finding, Pachacamac AS175 repeated on the Huacho pair UR233 + UR232 (Berlin VA63038b).
  • Two cases, and who sold them. A Pachacamac account copied at Huacho now appears twice, in Berlin (VA63038b) and in Basel (IVc.366.03). The Berlin museum’s online records (museum-digital, 305 khipus; data/smb/, not published) name Wilhelm Gretzer as the collector of all the Pachacamac sources: VA42508 (UR212), VA42510 (AS131), VA42518 (AS175) and also VA42559 and VA42601 (UR218, AS118). They name Eduard Gaffron for the Huacho khipus VA63038 (UR232, UR233), part of a Gaffron series VA63038–63044 that includes UR195 (VA63040, Huacho). For the Basel fragments the OKR also names Gaffron, whereas the Basel archive cited by Medrano (2022) names the ship’s doctor Arnold Masarey, who dug them near Huacho. If the OKR attribution is right, both Huacho copies reached Europe through Gaffron, who practised medicine in Lima from 1882 to 1923. If Medrano’s archive is right, they came through two different people. In either case the originals are Gretzer’s “Pachacamac” khipus. That an account from Pachacamac recurs twice at Huacho, about 150 km away, is either evidence that records travelled between the two places or a reason to doubt the Pachacamac attribution of these Gretzer khipus. The second reading has independent support. Gretzer obtained most of his objects from grave robbers and dug only occasionally, and most of his pieces carry the label “Pachacamac”; the museum bought about 40,000 of them in 1906–1907. Studies of his textiles caution that objects so labelled may have come from anywhere (Lambayeque-style textiles in the Ethnologisches Museum, Nuevo Mundo Mundos Nuevos, open access). He also collected at El Paraíso–Chuquitanta and elsewhere, and part of his collection went to Hannover, whose khipu MM001 is labelled “Huacho?”. The khipus themselves cannot decide it. Simple numeric features (value size, share of zeros, group size, subsidiaries, length) do not separate the khipus of the Pachacamac site museum (HP, 22) from those of Huacho without the copies (7): a nearest-centroid classifier gets 22 of 29 right, which is no better than always guessing the larger class (style_pach_huacho.py). We found no description of either link; Medrano describes MM008 in detail but does not compare it with other khipus.

The same scan finds that AS208 (Vienna IX/1961) and UR083 = KH0227 (Vienna 140708), both “Huaura Valley, Santa Rosalía hacienda”, are one khipu recorded twice under two numbers and read from opposite ends: 158 of 163 cords match in reverse order and 5 forward. It also finds AS003 (Heye 17/8827, 7 cords) repeating AS215 group 5 (Hood Museum, Nazca) in reverse order, 4, 10, 37, 7, 14, 4, with the top cord 76 = their sum after the group on both. On AS003 the pendants are reversed but the top cord is still listed after them, so the finding that top cords follow the group they sum (section 6) may partly reflect how top cords are recorded. On AS215 the Aschers' databook (pp. 127–132) shows this group of six, joined by the top cord, to be the summary part: each of its values is a sum over the four groups of the other part (4, 14, 7, 37, 10, 4), and the top cord 76 is their total. AS003 (Heye 17/8827, provenance unknown, 7 cords) therefore records only the summary of AS215 (Hood Museum 55-32-13302, Nazca), with its total. It is a second “totals only” record, after UR273B at Inkawasi. The Aschers do not mention AS003 in their AS215 entry.

Excerpts across the corpus

A corpus-wide search for groups of five or more cords repeated as a run of cords on a khipu with another museum number, in either direction and with at most one difference (excerpts.py), gives 189 hits against 1–11 with the values shifted by ±1 or ±2. Almost all are second recordings of one object. Two such pairs are not in our list of known duplicates. UR1107 (VA24370(B)) and UR237 (VA 66834) match on 26 of 29 cords in reverse order, with lengths within 3 cm. AS046 (Heye 17/565) and HP041 = KH0057 (MNAAHP 5693) match on 74 of 79 cords, with identical lengths. Besides MM015, the only link between two different khipus is the known pair AS114/AS123 (UR1114/UR1123; Urton 2005), recovered here without being looked for. AS123 group 5, 40, 9, 57, 30, 56, 80, 21, 8, 22, 12, is exactly the third ten-cord block of AS114 group 3. AS114 records its 16 blocks twice, in groups 3 and 4, and AS123 follows the first version. None of the other blocks of AS114 and none of the other groups of AS123 recurs elsewhere in the corpus (2 hits on small values, against 0–1 with shifted values). MM015 is therefore the only new excerpt the scan finds.

limitation

8. What the detectors do not find

The detectors look for sums and copies over contiguous cords, whole groups, strided groups and equal positions. At Puruchuco (UR060–UR083) they recover the matching pair UR066 ~ UR067 (9 equal values at the same positions, none at other offsets), but not the accounting hierarchy described by Urton and Brezine (2005): cords of one khipu equal sums over another no more often than on the offset baseline (404 against 402), and no khipu is the position-wise sum of two others. Relations built on colour-selected or non-contiguous cords are therefore outside what our scans can detect, and the absence of further links in the corpus should be read with this in mind.

Methods in brief

  • Cord value = sum of knot_value_type; subtree value adds all subsidiaries. Values are compared on pendants only, on subtrees, or both, as stated per test.
  • Every detector has a baseline built from the corpus itself: values shuffled within each khipu, record order shuffled, cluster order shuffled, annotation shifted circularly, or target values offset by ±d, chosen so that the baseline keeps the structure the test is not about. For sums over groups, value shuffling proved too permissive (it changes the distribution of group totals); the offset baseline is used there. Thresholds are the maximum over shuffled corpora unless a p-value is given.
  • Each detector was run on known cases before being applied to the corpus; the known-case results are in section 6.
